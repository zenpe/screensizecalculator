## What is PPI?

**PPI (Pixels Per Inch)** measures pixel density—how many pixels are packed into each inch of screen space. Higher PPI means sharper text, crisper images, and smoother edges.

**Why it matters**: Two monitors can be the same size but look completely different. A 27-inch 1080p monitor (82 PPI) shows visible pixels and grainy text, while a 27-inch 4K monitor (163 PPI) looks crystal clear.

## The PPI Problem: Real-World Examples

### Example 1: Why 27-Inch 1080p Looks Grainy

**Setup**: 27-inch monitor, 1920×1080 resolution

**PPI Calculation**:
- Diagonal: 27 inches
- Resolution: 1920×1080
- **PPI: 82**

**Result**: At typical 24-inch viewing distance, you can see individual pixels. Text looks fuzzy, images lack sharpness.

### Example 2: Why 27-Inch 4K Looks Sharp

**Setup**: 27-inch monitor, 3840×2160 resolution

**PPI Calculation**:
- Diagonal: 27 inches
- Resolution: 3840×2160
- **PPI: 163**

**Result**: Pixels are invisible at normal viewing distance. Text is razor-sharp, images look printed.

### The Difference

**Same screen size, 2× the PPI = dramatically better clarity**

| Monitor | Resolution | PPI | Visual Quality |
|---------|-----------|-----|----------------|
| 27" 1080p | 1920×1080 | 82 | ❌ Grainy, visible pixels |
| 27" 1440p | 2560×1440 | 109 | ✅ Sharp, excellent |
| 27" 4K | 3840×2160 | 163 | ✅ Crystal clear, retina-quality |

## How to Calculate PPI

### The Formula

```
PPI = √(Horizontal Pixels² + Vertical Pixels²) ÷ Diagonal Inches
```

### Step-by-Step Calculation

**Example**: 24-inch monitor, 1920×1080 resolution

**Step 1**: Square the horizontal pixels
- 1920² = 3,686,400

**Step 2**: Square the vertical pixels
- 1080² = 1,166,400

**Step 3**: Add them together
- 3,686,400 + 1,166,400 = 4,852,800

**Step 4**: Take the square root
- √4,852,800 = 2,203 diagonal pixels

**Step 5**: Divide by diagonal inches
- 2,203 ÷ 24 = **91.8 PPI**

## The PPI Sweet Spot

### Optimal PPI Range by Use Case

**Office Work & Productivity**:
- **Minimum**: 90 PPI (acceptable)
- **Optimal**: 100-120 PPI (comfortable)
- **Premium**: 130-150 PPI (luxury)

**Gaming**:
- **Minimum**: 90 PPI (playable)
- **Optimal**: 100-110 PPI (balanced performance)
- **Premium**: 120-140 PPI (visual quality priority)

**Professional Work** (photo/video editing, design):
- **Minimum**: 110 PPI (acceptable)
- **Optimal**: 140-160 PPI (excellent)
- **Premium**: 160-220 PPI (retina-class)

**Programming & Development**:
- **Minimum**: 100 PPI (readable)
- **Optimal**: 110-140 PPI (comfortable for long sessions)
- **Premium**: 150+ PPI (crisp code, multiple windows)

### The 110 PPI Rule

**110 PPI** is the magic number for most users:
- Text is sharp and readable
- No visible pixels at 20-24 inch viewing distance
- Doesn't require UI scaling
- Balanced GPU performance

**Configurations that hit ~110 PPI**:
- 24" @ 1440p (122 PPI)
- 27" @ 1440p (109 PPI)
- 32" @ 4K (138 PPI)
- 34" ultrawide @ 3440×1440 (110 PPI)

## Why PPI Matters More Than Resolution

### Resolution Alone Is Misleading

**Scenario**: Which monitor is sharper?

**Monitor A**: 24-inch, 1920×1080 (92 PPI)
**Monitor B**: 32-inch, 2560×1440 (92 PPI)

**Answer**: They look **equally sharp** because they have the same PPI, even though Monitor B has higher resolution.

### The Size-Resolution Relationship

**Key insight**: Larger screen + same resolution = lower PPI = less sharp

| Screen Size | 1080p PPI | 1440p PPI | 4K PPI |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **24"** | 92 | 122 | 184 |
| **27"** | 82 | 109 | 163 |
| **32"** | 69 | 92 | 138 |

**Notice**: 32" 1440p (92 PPI) = 24" 1080p (92 PPI) in sharpness

## Common PPI Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1: Buying Too Large at Low Resolution

**Example**: 32-inch 1080p monitor
- **PPI**: 69 (very low)
- **Problem**: Visible pixels, blurry text, poor image quality
- **Fix**: Get 32" 1440p or 4K instead

### ❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Viewing Distance

**Example**: 27" 4K monitor (163 PPI) at 36 inches away
- **Problem**: Sitting too far to see the benefit
- **Fix**: Either sit closer or save money with 1440p

### ❌ Mistake 3: Too High PPI Without Scaling

**Example**: 24" 4K monitor (184 PPI)
- **Problem**: Text and UI elements too small
- **Fix**: Use 150% scaling or choose 1440p

### ❌ Mistake 4: Mismatched Dual Monitors

**Example**: 24" 1080p (92 PPI) + 27" 1440p (109 PPI)
- **Problem**: Text size differs between screens
- **Fix**: Match PPI across monitors for consistency

## PPI and Viewing Distance

### The Visual Acuity Formula

**Human eye with 20/20 vision** can resolve:
- **1 arc minute** (1/60th of a degree)

**Minimum distance for invisible pixels**:
```
Distance = (Screen Height × 3438) ÷ Vertical Resolution
```

### PPI Distance Chart

| PPI | Minimum Distance | Comfortable Distance |
|-----|------------------|---------------------|
| **80** | 34 inches (86 cm) | 40+ inches (100+ cm) |
| **90** | 30 inches (76 cm) | 36+ inches (90+ cm) |
| **100** | 27 inches (69 cm) | 32+ inches (80+ cm) |
| **110** | 25 inches (64 cm) | 30+ inches (75+ cm) |
| **120** | 23 inches (58 cm) | 28+ inches (70+ cm) |
| **140** | 19 inches (48 cm) | 24+ inches (60+ cm) |
| **160** | 17 inches (43 cm) | 22+ inches (55+ cm) |

**How to use this**:
1. Measure your viewing distance
2. Find the PPI that makes pixels invisible at that distance
3. Choose monitor size + resolution to achieve that PPI

**Your setup**: Desk with 24-inch viewing distance

**Option 1**: 27" 1080p (82 PPI)

- You're at 24 inches (too close)
- **Result**: Visible pixels ❌

**Option 2**: 27" 1440p (109 PPI)

## PPI and UI Scaling

### When You Need Scaling

**PPI above 140**: UI elements become too small
**Solution**: Use display scaling

| PPI Range | Recommended Scaling | Example |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| **80-110** | 100% (no scaling) | 27" 1440p |
| **110-140** | 100-125% | 32" 4K |
| **140-180** | 125-150% | 27" 4K |
| **180-220** | 150-200% | 24" 4K |

### Scaling Trade-offs

**Pros**:
- Sharper text and images
- More screen real estate
- Better for high-DPI content

**Cons**:
- Some apps don't scale well
- Blurry text in non-optimized apps
- Inconsistent UI sizes

**Best practice**: Stick to 100-120 PPI to avoid scaling issues

### Method 1: Manual Calculation

1. Find your resolution (Settings → Display)
2. Find your screen size (measure diagonal or check specs)
3. Use the formula: `√(width² + height²) ÷ diagonal`

**Windows**:
- Right-click desktop → Display settings
- Check resolution and scale
- Calculate PPI manually

**macOS**:
- System Settings → Displays
- Check resolution
- Calculate PPI manually

**Linux**:
- Run: `xdpyinfo | grep resolution`
- Shows DPI (same as PPI for screens)
